Key Insights

The protocol is remarkably stable across all three configurations. Total energy per device: 52.2 mJ (79AS·20D) → 57.2 mJ (50AS·50D) → 69.5 mJ (20AS·79D). Despite varying the device count by 4× (20→79) and the AS count by 4× (79→20), total energy shifts by only 17.30 mJ (33.1%).
Enrollment cost follows AS-to-device ratio. With 79 AS for 20 devices (ratio 3.95 AS/device), enrollment is 26.2 mJ. At 1:1 ratio (50AS·50D) it drops to 24.8 mJ. At 0.25:1 (20AS·79D, ~4 devices per AS) it rises to 27.4 mJ — more queuing and retransmissions when each AS is busier.
Authentication and Key Exchange costs rise as AS nodes decrease. Fewer AS nodes means each one handles more devices concurrently, increasing CoAP wait times. Auth energy: 16.7 → 17.7 → 17.9 mJ. KeyEx energy: 12.1 → 13.2 → 14.3 mJ. The 20AS config pays the highest per-round cost due to contention.
Sweet spot: 50 AS / 50 Devices gives the best overall balance. It has the lowest enrollment cost (24.8 mJ) and moderate auth/keyex overhead — because 1 device per AS eliminates enrollment queuing while keeping the RPL network manageable.
Load distribution summary: 79AS·20D → 0.25 devices/AS (most AS nodes idle) | 50AS·50D → 1.0 device/AS (perfectly balanced) | 20AS·79D → ~4 devices/AS (highest contention). The protocol handles all three cases gracefully, confirming good scalability.
