\chapter{Conclusion}

\begin{singlespace}
This report presented a lightweight anonymous authentication scheme for multihop
IoT networks that extends DAuth~\cite{das2026comsnets} with two additions:
per-session pseudonym rotation to hide $ID_D$ from the open channel, and a dual-state
mechanism to recover from desynchronisation without re-enrolment.

Formal verification in ProVerif confirms all 10 security queries under the Dolev--Yao
model, covering authentication correspondence, session key secrecy, forward secrecy,
anonymity, and offline guessing resistance.

COOJA\slash Contiki-NG simulations on 100-node networks show per-device energy
reductions of \textbf{9.5\,\%} over LAAKA and \textbf{32.4\,\%} over Zhou et al.
The dual-state mechanism is particularly significant in the desynchronised case:
a single lost key-exchange packet forces DAuth into a full re-enrolment
(134.8\,mJ per recovery round), whereas the proposed scheme recovers through its
dual-state lookup at 87.2\,mJ, saving \textbf{35.3\,\%} in per-device recovery
energy (and the same in CPU time) over DAuth.

Hardware experiments on Raspberry Pi~4B confirm that the proposed scheme attains
virtually identical per-round cost to the non-anonymous DAuth baseline (within
\textbf{1\,\%}), while reducing energy by approximately \textbf{36\,\%} over LAAKA
and \textbf{43\,\%} over Zhou et al., demonstrating that the anonymity guarantee is
achieved at no measurable overhead on real hardware.

Among the four compared schemes, the proposed scheme is the only one to simultaneously
provide device anonymity, desynchronisation recovery, and decoupled distributed
authentication at overhead suitable for resource-constrained IoT devices.
\end{singlespace}

\section{Future Work}

Reducing the dual-state storage overhead at the authentication server while
preserving desynchronisation resilience is a promising direction for further
optimisation.
The present scheme also does not conceal the authentication server identity, as
$ID_{AS}$ appears in open-channel messages.
Extending pseudonym rotation to authentication servers would provide full
end-to-end identity unlinkability across the multihop network.
